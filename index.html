Inside the machine

See the hardware before you write the code

A superconducting quantum processor hangs at the bottom of a dilution refrigerator, a few thousandths of a degree above absolute zero. The course is organised around the same stages: each module sits one plate further down, from 300 K to the mixing chamber at 10 mK.

  • Microwave pulses travel down coaxial lines to steer each qubit.
  • Every gold stage is colder than the one above, filtering out heat and noise.
  • The mixing chamber cools the qubit chip to around 10 millikelvin.

Move your pointer over the machine to look around it.

Questions, answered

Do I need a physics background?

No. The course assumes high-school algebra and a little comfort with complex numbers. Module 01, the math toolkit, builds the linear algebra you need, and everything else is built up from there.

How do I complete a lesson?

Read the lesson, try its labs, then take the ten-question check. Get seven or more right on the first try and the lesson is marked complete in your module. You can retake any check with the options shuffled.

Where is my progress saved?

Only in your browser. Lesson progress, quiz scores and the reasoning notes you write are stored on your device, and nothing is sent anywhere.

Who is this for?

Students, engineers and researchers who want a real understanding of quantum computing — from first principles all the way to fault-tolerant architectures.
